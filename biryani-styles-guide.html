Biryani Styles, Kacchi & Pakki

There is no single biryani. Across South Asia, cities and families have shaped their own, and two words, kacchi and pakki, describe the biggest difference.

१

Kacchi: raw and patient

In a kacchi biryani, the meat is marinated raw with yogurt and spices and placed at the bottom of the pot. Partly cooked rice goes on top, the pot is sealed, and everything cooks together on gentle heat. It takes skill to time, but the meat and rice share every bit of flavour.

२

Pakki: cooked first

In a pakki biryani, the meat or vegetables are cooked into a gravy first, then layered with parboiled rice and finished on dum. It is more forgiving for home cooks and gives a richer, saucier base under fluffy rice.

३

Regional styles

A few well-loved examples:

  • Hyderabadi: often kacchi, bold with chilli, mint and fried onions.
  • Lucknowi (Awadhi): pakki, delicate and fragrant, gently spiced.
  • Kolkata: lighter, perfumed, famous for its potato and egg.
  • Malabar and Dindigul: short-grain rices and southern spices.
४

Choosing your rice

Most northern styles use aged long-grain basmati, which stays separate and grows long when cooked. Aged rice absorbs water evenly and breaks less. Southern styles often use small, fragrant grains such as jeerakasala or seeraga samba.

५

Vegetable biryani

A vegetable biryani follows the same pakki method with a mix of vegetables, paneer or mushrooms cooked in a spiced yogurt gravy. Fried onions, mint and saffron matter just as much, so treat it with the same care.
