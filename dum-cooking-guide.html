Cooking on Dum

Dum means breath. In dum cooking, a sealed pot traps steam so the rice, meat and spices finish slowly in their own fragrant breath.

१

Parboil the rice

Boil the soaked rice in plenty of salted water with whole spices until it is about 70 percent cooked: long, still firm in the centre, and breaking with a little resistance. It will finish cooking in the sealed pot.

२

Layer with care

Build the pot from the bottom up:

  • Marinated meat or cooked gravy at the base.
  • Rice in one or two loose layers on top.
  • Fried onions, mint, coriander, saffron milk and a little ghee between and above.
३

Seal the pot

Traditionally the lid is sealed with a rope of dough, so no steam can escape. A tight lid with a sheet of foil underneath works well at home. The trapped steam is what cooks the top layers and carries the aroma through the rice.

४

Gentle heat

Cook on high heat for a few minutes to build steam, then turn it very low. Placing the pot on a flat griddle (tawa) spreads the heat and protects the bottom from burning. Most biryanis need 20 to 40 minutes on dum, depending on the style.

५

Rest, then open

Turn off the heat and leave the pot sealed for ten minutes. Open it at the table if you can, for the burst of fragrance. Use a wide spoon to lift from the side, bringing up both rice and meat so every plate gets each layer.
